\chapter{Introduction}
\label{Chapter 1}

\section{Introduction}
Retail lending in India has grown dramatically over the last decade. Personal loans, two-wheeler loans, consumer-durable loans, gold loans, credit-card revolving balances, salary advances and a long tail of buy-now-pay-later style products are now offered by a mix of public-sector banks, private banks, small finance banks, non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) and a rapidly multiplying set of digital-only fintech lenders. The flip side of growth in originations is growth in delinquencies. A small but non-trivial percentage of every disbursed cohort fails to pay an installment on or before the due date, and the lender is obliged by Reserve Bank of India (RBI) policy and by basic prudence to chase those installments before the account is allowed to slide into the non-performing-asset bucket.

The chasing of overdue installments is what the industry calls ``collections''. Once an account is more than one day past due (DPD), it enters a structured pipeline of follow-up actions whose intensity and tone escalate with the number of days the account remains unpaid. The classical bucketing in Indian retail collections is shown in Table~\ref{tab:dpd-buckets}.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Collection buckets typical to Indian retail-lending portfolios.}
\label{tab:dpd-buckets}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
\textbf{Bucket} & \textbf{DPD range} & \textbf{Typical activity} \\
\midrule
Bucket 0 & 1--30 days   & Soft reminders, automated calls, light SMS \\
Bucket 1 & 31--60 days  & Tele-calling, WhatsApp, agent calls \\
Bucket 2 & 61--90 days  & Field visits, legal notice preparation \\
Bucket 3 & 91--120 days & Pre-write-off, recovery escalation \\
NPA      & 120+ days    & Settlement, legal action \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

For most retail products the recovery rate falls off sharply as the bucket deepens. A loan collected in Bucket~0 is recovered cheaply, requires almost no negotiation and does not need a provisioning hit on the lender's balance sheet; a loan that drifts into Bucket~3 or beyond is materially more expensive, often requires settlement at a discount and erodes the customer relationship. The economic case for early-bucket recovery is therefore overwhelming. Operationally, however, early-bucket follow-up is hard at scale: a lender with a hundred thousand active retail loans can easily have eight to twelve thousand accounts in Bucket~0 on any given day, and each needs a contact attempt within a tight cycle. Lenders either build large in-house collections teams or, more commonly, outsource the recovery work in monthly batches to specialised collection agencies who keep a percentage of the recovered amount as commission.

The internship that produced the work documented in this report was carried out at Ignosis, a fintech infrastructure company best known in the Account Aggregator (AA) ecosystem. Over the last eighteen months, the firm has layered a collections suite onto its existing AA platform, reusing the same multi-tenant infrastructure and integrating AA-pulled bank-statement data into collection prioritisation. I joined as a comprehensive-project intern on 1 January 2026 and worked full-time through 31 May 2026, contributing to the orchestrator backend and dashboards as well as to a separate automation toolkit containing the WhatsApp service, the desktop client and the Android SMS sender. The title of this comprehensive project is \textbf{``AI-Based Collections Platform''}; the platform is a multi-tenant web application that provides a case management dashboard, a bulk campaign module, an autonomous AI voice calling engine, multi-channel outreach automation across WhatsApp and SMS, and analytics on contact rates, agent productivity, call dispositions and recovery percentages.

\section{Motivation}
The motivation for this project sits at the intersection of an industry pain point and a technology opening. On the industry side, every percentage point of recovery in Bucket 0 directly improves the lender's Gross Non-Performing Asset (GNPA) metric, reduces provisioning and saves real money. A platform that lifts the early-bucket collection rate from a typical 60--70 percent to the mid-80s -- the target that the Ignosis customer base has set for itself -- is materially valuable to the lender. On the technology side, three developments have made an AI-driven collections platform far more practical than it would have been even three years ago:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Conversational voice models.} Speech-to-text accuracy on Indian-English and on the major Indian regional languages has crossed the threshold at which an AI agent can hold an outbound collection conversation that actually moves the needle. The firm's internal voice AI platform offers programmable voice agents with low enough latency that customers do not notice they are not speaking to a human.
\item \textbf{Account Aggregator data.} The AA framework allows a lender to fetch the customer's bank statements with explicit consent. This pulls the conversation forward from ``please pay'' to ``we can see your salary was credited yesterday, what is a convenient EMI date for you'' -- a far more effective prompt.
\item \textbf{Cheap programmatic SIMs.} It is now feasible to operate a bank of consumer-grade SIM cards in a software-controlled rotation, sending WhatsApp messages through real phone numbers and SMS through real Android devices. This bypasses the deliverability ceiling of business APIs.
\end{enumerate}

Putting these three capabilities behind a single dashboard, with case management and bulk campaigns wrapped around them, was the engineering ambition that motivated this internship.

\section{Objective}
The concrete objectives of the project, all of which were either fully or partially achieved during the internship, are:
\begin{itemize}
\item To develop a centralised case management interface, accessible to authenticated collection agents over the browser, that lists assigned cases, surfaces customer payment history and previous interaction notes, and lets the agent act on each case.
\item To implement a bulk campaign module that lets a campaign manager wrap a batch of cases -- typically a CSV uploaded at the start of the cycle -- into a single executable unit and schedule it for execution.
\item To integrate AI-powered voice calling into the campaign module so that customer outreach can continue outside human-agent working hours.
\item To build a multi-channel follow-up layer over WhatsApp and SMS that uses real SIMs rather than business APIs, with anti-block heuristics and automatic device rotation.
\item To analyse roughly forty thousand customer records using Python and pandas, identify behavioural patterns relevant to prioritisation, and feed the insights back into case-load allocation.
\item To follow the production engineering practices of the host company throughout: code review, unit and integration testing, conventional commits, ticketed work items and zero-downtime deployments.
\end{itemize}

\section{Problem Statement}
The collections function for a typical Indian retail lender, in the absence of a centralised software platform, suffers from a small number of structural problems that compound on each other:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Manual case allocation.} The lender hands a CSV or Excel file of delinquent accounts to the agency at the start of every monthly cycle. The agency manager then distributes those cases by hand across the agents on the floor. The mapping is opaque to the lender, hard to audit and hard to reproduce.
\item \textbf{Single-channel outreach.} Agents reach for the desk phone first; if the customer does not pick up the phone they may or may not also send a SMS. There is no orchestrated follow-up across phone, WhatsApp and SMS, which means many customers are never reached on the channel they are likely to respond on.
\item \textbf{Agent idle time.} Agents spend a significant fraction of their working day searching for the next case in a spreadsheet, dialling a number manually and reading the customer's history before each call. The actual conversation is only a fraction of the working minute.
\item \textbf{No after-hours coverage.} A human agent's working day ends at 6 PM or 7 PM. After that the lender effectively has no contact channel even though many salaried customers are most reachable in the late evening.
\item \textbf{No structured measurement.} Recovery rates are reported as a single number at month-end. Lenders cannot inspect bucket-level conversion, channel-level effectiveness or agent-level productivity in any meaningful way.
\item \textbf{Single-attempt deliverability.} WhatsApp and SMS gateways available through business APIs throttle aggressively, charge per message and are subject to template-approval delays. SIM-based outreach from real consumer-grade numbers has materially higher deliverability but no off-the-shelf platform exposes that capability to the collections team.
\end{enumerate}

The problem statement of this project, in compact form, can therefore be stated as:

\textit{``Design and contribute to a centralised, multi-tenant collections platform that improves contact rate, agent throughput and after-hours coverage by integrating AI voice calling and multi-channel outreach across WhatsApp and SMS into a single case management workflow, while preserving end-to-end auditability and supporting bulk campaign execution at the scale of a typical monthly allocation.''}

\section{Approach}
The approach taken on this project was iterative and code-first. Rather than write a long design document up-front and then implement it, the engineering team at Ignosis follows a plan-first-but-small workflow: each piece of work begins as a short Markdown plan attached to a Jira ticket, which is reviewed by the lead engineer (Mr.~Rahi Shah in my case), is then translated into a branch with conventional commits, and is shipped to a development environment within a few days. Production deployment happens after code review, integration testing in a staging tenant and verification by the QA team. Every artefact -- the plan, the branch, the merge request, the QA notes -- is preserved in the repository so that the entire decision trail is auditable months later. This approach is the dominant cultural fact about how the work was done, and it explains why the contributions described in this report are spread across several Git branches that build on one another rather than concentrated in a single feature branch.

\section{Scope of the Project}
The scope of the work undertaken during the internship covers the following:
\begin{itemize}
\item Backend contributions to the multi-tenant orchestrator monolith, including data entities, REST controllers, services, scheduled jobs and schema migrations.
\item Frontend contributions to the agent dashboard for case management, campaign execution and analytics.
\item A separate automation toolkit that contains the WhatsApp service, the desktop application, the Android SMS sender app and an internal REST API server.
\item A one-off analysis on customer-level transaction and repayment data.
\end{itemize}

The scope explicitly excludes the AI voice agent itself (provided by the firm's internal voice AI platform), the telephony gateway, the cloud infrastructure provisioning, the lender's internal LMS and the regulatory compliance review.
