\begin{justify}
This report is the write-up of a five-month engineering internship at Ignosis, an Ahmedabad-based fintech infrastructure company. I joined in January and stayed through May. What we built (and what I contributed to) is an AI-Based Collections Platform that several Indian banks, NBFCs and digital lenders now use to chase overdue retail loans.

A short word about why this matters. Collections in Indian retail lending is still a stubbornly manual business. Each month the lender drops a fresh list of delinquent accounts on a recovery agency, sometimes a few thousand of them. Agents then have roughly thirty days to dial each customer and persuade them to pay. The agency earns only when it recovers, which means contact rate, agent throughput and being on the right channel at the right moment effectively decide whether the month is profitable. Without proper software, agencies cannot scale and lenders cannot really see what is working.

Our system is a single web app built around three interlocking pieces. At its core sits a tenant-configurable workflow engine -- a small graph of action nodes joined by boolean transitions -- which captures each lender's collection policy as data rather than as buried business logic. Sitting on top is the campaign module, which bundles thousands of cases into one executable batch and pushes them through the workflow. The third piece is the AI voice calling engine, plugged into the firm's internal voice AI platform and exposed to the rest of the system as just another action node. That last detail is what makes after-hours coverage even possible. Wrapping everything is a multi-channel automation layer that pushes WhatsApp from real consumer SIMs and dispatches SMS from a small Android app written for exactly this purpose.

The work I personally contributed: the case detail and case listing pages on the agent dashboard, the end-to-end campaign creation and CSV upload flow, the workflow nodes that wire AI calling into a running campaign, the speed dialer that pops the next case the instant an agent files a disposition, the WhatsApp service with its multi-device rotation and anti-ban heuristics, the desktop wrapper that hands all of this to non-technical operators, and the Android SMS sender. As of this submission the platform is recovering roughly 85 percent of Bucket~0 cases in production, and roughly 70 percent of the entire monthly allocation lands inside the first five days. Both numbers sit comfortably above the agency-only baseline the lenders had been running with before.
\end{justify}
