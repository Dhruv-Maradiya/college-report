The WhatsApp sender pool is the most operationally sensitive sub-system of the platform. Bans are routine; a sender that has been heavily used for a few weeks will eventually be flagged and require either a cool-down period or replacement. Best practice that emerged from the data is to introduce new numbers into the pool with a low daily cap (typically thirty messages per day) for the first week, gradually raise the cap over the next three weeks, and only bring the number to its full cap of one hundred and fifty after a month of warm-up. The block detection logic was the second improvement that made a measurable difference. Before the explicit error-substring matching and the soft-failure handling were added, the median time from a ban to the operator noticing it was about eighteen hours. After the changes, the orchestrator removes a banned sender from rotation within thirty seconds of the first banned-class error and reassigns the orphan contacts immediately. The AI voice agent's performance is measured along three dimensions: latency, contact rate and disposition accuracy. The latency budget that Bolna advertises was largely upheld in production: the median turn-end-to-agent-start latency, measured on the warmer half of the turn distribution, was 690 milliseconds. The slower tail, however, is materially worse -- the 95th-percentile latency is 1.4 seconds and the 99th-percentile crosses 2.2 seconds. This tail is dominated by ASR cold starts and is being investigated as part of the next release cycle. The disposition accuracy is the share of AI-call outcomes that, when randomly sampled and reviewed by the QA team, are classified correctly. The QA portal -- which is itself a contribution that landed in production during the internship period -- samples five percent of all AI calls and routes them to a QA reviewer who relistens to the recording, reads the LLM-generated transcript and either confirms or overrides the disposition. The QA-confirmed accuracy across roughly six thousand audited calls is 91 percent. The most common disagreement type is the AI agent classifying an outcome as a Promise to Pay when the customer was actually evasive about a specific date, which is a known training data gap and is being addressed by an upstream prompt change. A complete result analysis is incomplete without a candid record of the failure modes that the team encountered in production. The three most consequential were: An early version of the Apache Solr client pool did not configure an idle-connection eviction policy. After a few days of running, the ECS task would exhaust its file descriptors and the Solr requests would begin failing. The fix was a pooled connection manager with a maximum of ten thousand connections, a thirty-second idle timeout and a five-minute eviction interval, and a hook to close the pool cleanly. Bolna posts a webhook at call termination, but the status-poll job (the original mechanism for fetching outcomes) was still being scheduled. When both arrived in the same second, the second one would attempt to advance the case from a state that the first had already moved past, leading to spurious workflow errors. The fix was to cancel the in-flight poll job as part of the webhook handling, and to set the field before any downstream node executes so that the existing guard fires for the laggard. The orchestrator's parser threw, every voice metric on the workflow-poll path went dark, and we did not notice for a few hours because the webhook path was unaffected. The fix was a tolerant parser plus a Sentry alert that fires on any schema break in Bolna's payload. Each of these incidents resulted in a runbook entry, a test case and a Liquibase or configuration change that prevents recurrence. Beyond the numbers, the qualitative feedback from the stakeholders involved in the deployment was uniformly positive. The collection-agency floor managers reported that the speed dialer collapsed agent ramp-up time from a typical four weeks to under two, because new agents no longer needed to learn the dashboard navigation tricks that the speed dialer renders unnecessary. The lender-side operations team valued the in-product analytics: prior to the platform, recovery percentages were a black box and there was no objective basis on which to evaluate whether the agency was performing well; the dashboard's bucket-level and agent-level breakdowns made that conversation tractable for the first time. The compliance team valued the call-frequency guard (an explicit pre-call check that the RBI's fair-practices code requirement on the number of attempts to a delinquent customer is not violated) because it removed an entire category of audit findings from the lender's quarterly review. In short, while there are clearly more wins to be had -- the AI voice agent's tail latency, the WhatsApp ban probability for new numbers, and the still-manual nature of the lender-side allocation upload all warrant further engineering -- the platform in its current state has been a material step forward against every metric that the project set out to improve.

This report has documented a six-month engineering internship at Ignosis during which I contributed to a production multi-tenant AI-Based Collections Platform. The platform is now in active deployment across four lender tenants and is materially improving the recovery percentages that those lenders have historically been able to achieve. The internship took me from being an undergraduate with a working knowledge of Java and React to being an engineer comfortable shipping production code into a mature multi-tenant Spring Boot monolith, a UmiJS dashboard, a Node.js automation library and an Electron desktop client. The breadth of the platform meant that I had to learn the WhatsApp multi-device protocol and the Baileys library, the Bolna voice-agent integration model, the MVEL2 expression language and its role in our workflow engine, Liquibase migrations, Keycloak realm modelling, Apache Solr's eDisMax parser and the Ant Design Pro component library. Each of these was new at the start of the year and is, at the end of it, something I can use productively. Equally importantly the internship taught me the operational disciplines of production engineering: writing tests that exercise the real database rather than mocks, writing plans before code, writing Sentry alerts before incidents, designing for tenant isolation by default rather than retrofitting it under pressure. The headline result of the work is captured in Table~ of Chapter~4: the Bucket 0 collection rate moved from 64 percent to 85 percent, the percentage of cases recovered within the first five days of a cycle moved from 38 percent to 70 percent, and the multi-channel cumulative contact rate reached 96 percent. These numbers are a team product rather than an individual product, but the modules I contributed -- the case detail and case listing pages, the bulk campaign module, the speed dialer, the WhatsApp automation library, the Electron desktop client and the Android SMS sender -- are direct enablers of each of those numbers. The project also met every one of the nine engineering problems identified at the end of Chapter~2: cycle-level case management, comprehensive case detail, bulk campaign execution, the speed dialer, AI voice calling, WhatsApp automation, SMS automation, auditability and multi-tenant isolation.
