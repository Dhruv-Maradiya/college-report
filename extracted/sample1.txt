This report documents a comprehensive project carried out during a full-time engineering internship at Ignosis, a fintech infrastructure company in Ahmedabad. The brief of the project was to design and contribute to an AI-Based Collections Platform that is being used by banks, non-banking financial companies and digital-lending fintechs in India for the recovery of overdue retail loan accounts. In its current form the collections process for an Indian retail lender is largely manual. Each month a fresh allocation of delinquent cases -- often running to several thousand customers per agency -- is handed over by the lender, and a team of human agents calls, follows up and persuades each customer over a roughly thirty-day cycle. The agency is paid only on recovery, which means the model is exquisitely sensitive to contact rate, agent productivity and the speed with which the right customer is reached on the right channel. Without a centralised software platform, agencies struggle to scale and lenders cannot measure performance objectively. The platform that this project contributed to addresses these gaps through a single web-based application that integrates a case management dashboard for human agents, a bulk campaign module that wraps thousands of customer cases into a single executable unit, an AI voice calling engine (built on the Bolna conversational voice provider and the Exotel telephony gateway) that runs day and night without human agents in the loop, and a multi-channel automation layer that sends WhatsApp and SMS messages from real consumer-grade SIMs to maximise the deliverability that business-class APIs cannot offer. The backend is a multi-tenant Spring Boot 3.2 monolith, the dashboard is a React application built with the UmiJS framework and the Ant Design Pro component library, and the off-platform automation tooling is a TypeScript / Node.js monorepo packaged as both a standalone CLI and an Electron desktop application. The contributions described in this report span the case detail and case listing pages of the agent dashboard; the end-to-end campaign creation, CSV upload and execution pipeline; the speed-dialer that auto-loads the next case after each call disposition is submitted; the WhatsApp automation service built on the Baileys protocol library with multi-device rotation, micro-variation message templating and proactive block-avoidance heuristics; the Electron desktop wrapper that exposes these capabilities to non-technical agents; the Android SMS sender mobile app built with React Native and a custom native module that uses the on-device SIM; and the integration of these channels with the central case management backend. A small Python-pandas analysis project on roughly forty thousand customer records is also reported. The platform is reaching an early-bucket collection rate of about eighty-five percent in production and is recovering close to seventy percent of allocated cases within the first five days of each monthly cycle, which represents a material improvement over the agency-only baseline that the lenders were previously operating with.

Retail lending in India has grown dramatically over the last decade. Personal loans, two-wheeler loans, consumer-durable loans, gold loans, credit-card revolving balances, salary advances and a long tail of buy-now-pay-later style products are now offered by a mix of public-sector banks, private banks, small finance banks, non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) and a rapidly multiplying set of digital-only fintech lenders. The flip side of growth in originations is growth in delinquencies. A small but non-trivial percentage of every disbursed cohort fails to pay an installment on or before the due date, and the lender is obliged by Reserve Bank of India (RBI) policy and by basic prudence to chase those installments before the account is allowed to slide into the non-performing-asset bucket. The chasing of overdue installments is what the industry calls ``collections''. Once an account is more than one day past due (DPD), it enters a structured pipeline of follow-up actions whose intensity and tone escalate with the number of days the account remains unpaid. The classical bucketing in Indian retail collections is shown in Table~ . [h] lll & & Bucket 0 & 1--30 days & Soft reminders, automated calls, light SMS Bucket 1 & 31--60 days & Tele-calling, WhatsApp, agent calls Bucket 2 & 61--90 days & Field visits, legal notice preparation Bucket 3 & 91--120 days & Pre-write-off, recovery escalation NPA & 120+ days & Settlement, legal action For most retail products, the recovery rate falls off sharply as the bucket deepens. A loan that is collected in Bucket 0 is recovered cheaply, requires almost no negotiation, does not need any provisioning hit on the lender's balance sheet and rarely produces a customer complaint. A loan that drifts into Bucket 3 or beyond is materially more expensive to recover, often requires settlement at a discount, consumes legal team hours and erodes the customer relationship. The economic case for early-bucket recovery is therefore overwhelming, and every lender invests disproportionate effort in trying to get accounts cured as early in their delinquency life as possible. Operationally, however, early-bucket follow-up is hard at scale. A lender with a hundred thousand active retail loans can easily have eight to twelve thousand accounts in Bucket 0 on any given day. Each of those accounts needs a contact attempt -- a phone call, a SMS reminder, a WhatsApp nudge, sometimes a personal visit -- within a tight cycle so that the customer pays before the next cut-off date and the bucket statistics do not deteriorate. To do this, lenders either build large in-house collections teams or, more commonly, outsource the recovery work in monthly batches to specialised collection agencies who keep a percentage of the recovered amount as their commission. The collections function for a typical Indian retail lender, in the absence of a centralised software platform, suffers from a small number of structural problems that compound on each other: The lender hands a CSV or Excel file of delinquent accounts to the agency at the start of every monthly cycle. The agency manager then distributes those cases by hand across the agents on the floor. The mapping is opaque to the lender, hard to audit and hard to reproduce. Agents reach for the desk phone first; if the customer does not pick up the phone they may or may not also send a SMS. There is no orchestrated follow-up across phone, WhatsApp and SMS, which means many customers are never reached on the channel they are likely to respond on. Agents spend a significant fraction of their working day searching for the next case in a spreadsheet, dialling a number manually and reading the customer's history before each call. The actual conversation is only a fraction of the working minute. A human agent's working day ends at 6 PM or 7 PM. After that the lender effectively has no contact channel even though many salaried customers are most reachable in the late evening. Recovery rates are reported as a single number at month-end. Lenders cannot inspect bucket-level conversion, channel-level effectiveness or agent-level productivity in any meaningful way. WhatsApp and SMS gateways available through business APIs throttle aggressively, charge per message and are subject to template-approval delays. SIM-based outreach from real consumer-grade numbers has materially higher deliverability but no off-the-shelf platform exposes that capability to the collections team. The problem statement of this project, in compact form, can therefore be stated as: The motivation for this project sits at the intersection of an industry pain point and a technology opening. On the industry side, every percentage point of recovery in Bucket 0 directly improves the lender's Gross Non-Performing Asset (GNPA) metric, reduces provisioning and saves real money. A platform that lifts the early-bucket collection rate from a typical 60--70 percent to the mid-80s -- the target that the Ignosis customer base has set for itself -- is materially valuable to the lender. On the technology side, three developments have made an AI-driven collections platform far more practical than it would have been even three years ago: Speech-to-text accuracy on Indian-English and on the major Indian regional languages has crossed the threshold at which an AI agent can hold an outbound collection conversation that actually moves the needle. Providers such as Bolna (the partner used in this project) and Smallest.ai now offer programmable voice agents with low enough latency that customers do not notice they are not speaking to a human. The AA framework allows a lender to fetch the customer's bank statements with explicit consent. This pulls the conversation forward from ``please pay'' to ``we can see your salary was credited yesterday, what is a convenient EMI date for you'' -- a far more effective prompt. It is now feasible to operate a bank of consumer-grade SIM cards in a software-controlled rotation, sending WhatsApp messages through real phone numbers and SMS through real Android devices. This bypasses the deliverability ceiling of business APIs. Putting these three capabilities behind a single dashboard, with case management and bulk campaigns wrapped around them, was the engineering ambition that motivated this internship. The concrete objectives of the project, all of which were either fully or partially achieved during the internship, are: To develop a centralised case management interface, accessible to authenticated collection agents over the browser, that lists assigned cases, surfaces customer payment history and previous interaction notes, and lets the agent act on each case. To implement a bulk campaign module that lets a campaign manager wrap a batch of cases -- typically a CSV uploaded at the start of the cycle -- into a single executable unit and schedule it for execution. To integrate AI-powered voice calling into the campaign module so that customer outreach can continue outside human-agent working hours. To build a multi-channel follow-up layer over WhatsApp and SMS that uses real SIMs rather than business APIs, with anti-block heuristics and automatic device rotation. To analyse roughly forty thousand customer records using Python and pandas, identify behavioural patterns relevant to prioritisation, and feed the insights back into case-load allocation. To follow the production engineering practices of the host company throughout: code review, unit and integration testing, conventional commits, ticketed work items and zero-downtime deployments.
